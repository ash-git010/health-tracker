Thank you everyone for the help. So i read the comment section and did a bunch of research on the best fitness watches. I guess I will share it here in case someone is in a similar situation. I think the biggest mistake people make is looking for the best one overall. There really isn't one. It depends a lot on what you actually want to track. Someone who just wants steps, sleep, and basic health data doesn't need to spend $500. On the other hand, someone who trains 5-6 days a week, cares about recovery, HR zones, GPS accuracy, and performance metrics will probably outgrow a basic tracker pretty quickly. This is how I would divide them by price and use case:

Best fitness trackers overall

Garmin Fenix / Epix / high-end Forerunner models - There is basically a consensus that Garmin makes the best fitness watches, regarding of the price point. These particular series are made of better materials, they have a long battery life, advanced training metrics, recovery tracking, extremely accurate GPS, and various sports-specific features. For runners, hikers, triathletes, endurance athletes, etc, these are some of the best options available.

Apple Watch - Second best smartwatch, but not necessarily the best fitness tracker. The health features are excellent though. It has heart rate monitoring, ECG, sleep tracking, workout tracking, fall detection, activity rings. The main problem is battery life. For iPhone users who want one device that does everything, it is probably the best choice.

Best mid-range fitness watches

Garmin Vivosmart / Vivoactive / Forerunner entry models - Again garmin, which is not a surprise. Not as good as the other series mentioned above, but these have training load, recovery information, heart rate zones, gps tracking, running metrics, cycling and swimming data, etc. Battery life is also excellent compared to most smartwatches.

Samsung Galaxy Watch - A good option if you are already in the Android ecosystem. The biggest advantage is that it feels more like a smartwatch while still having fitness features. The downside is battery life. Compared to Garmin, charging every day or two can feel annoying. But it's a solid choice if you want a smartwatch first and fitness tracker second.

Best budget fitness trackers

Xiaomi Smart Band series - Probably the best value option for most casual users. You get all the basics, and the battery life is great. The downside is that the data is not always as detailed or polished as more expensive devices. The app experience is also not as refined.

Fitbit Inspire / Charge series - Not the best, but the app is very simple to understand, and it's a solid ecosystem for beginners.

Hi everyone, I have tried Hevy, Strong, Jefit which I found quite simple and a bit restrictive, with pay walled features. There are two standouts imho, both at the opposite ends of the spectrum:

Fitnotes - genuinely free and fully functional tracker. Great if you want the basics.

Liftosaur - An excellent system, huge props to the developer. It offers a deeply customisable experience, but some basic coding knowledge required, and to build something truly "Intellegent" requires a quite a bit. A few people I know have struggled to get over the learning curve, but its well worth checking out. Also has several features pay walled including graphs and timers, which is a bit of a shame.

By background I am a medical doctor and data scientist, and I felt there was space to build an app which had depth and breadth, backed by science, and coached based on learning the user, but wasn't built behind a paywall. So over some time, as a passion project, I have built RepMD, with input from experienced gym athletes, because I wanted a training app that felt practical, transparent, and useful in the gym, and I am aware there are already a lot of apps in this space.

My hope is that RepMD can help people log training quickly, understand their progress clearly, and personalise their programming without being forced into a black-box coaching system. I have built the app for lifters, hybrid athletes, and recentlybadded a trainer feature so you can have multiple profiles.

The app can be anything from a simple tracker to a full coach, learning profiles for every exercise and muscle group, giving tailored suggestions for every workout. It has a wide range of features from program + mesocycle generation, strength standards, detailed analytics, plate and set calculators, scheduled and automatic deload periods, and more.

There is a lot of customisation, which I appreciate can be a double edged sword, but at the heart of it I want the user to have control. You can adjust progression style, effort targets, deload rules, fatigue and recovery behaviour, equipment availability and increments, rest timers, reminders, and how much guidance the app shows. You can keep it simple, or expose more advanced controls, its up to you.

As a coach app can suggest load, reps, sets, and rest, but the aim is to explain the reasoning rather than just say “trust the algorithm.” It can learn how different exercises behave for you, how muscles are responding, where stimulus is coming from, and when it may make sense to push, hold, deload, or return gently.

RepMD also includes a paid Trainer Mode upgrade (£19.99) for personal trainers managing multiple clients. We also support backup/import/export tools for your data, so your training history is not trapped, and can manage imports from other apps, so you don't loose progress.

RepMD is free to use, supported by a small banner ad, and there is a one-time purchase to remove ads (£9.99). It works on your device, no cloud account required. It also supports health connect and apple health, and has a handy home screen widget to keep you updated.

The app is available on both Apple (https://apps.apple.com/gb/app/repmd-workout-coach/id6760118314 ) and Android (https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=uk.sadlerstudios.repmd ). I am always looking to improve the app, and would welcome any thoughts and feedback.
